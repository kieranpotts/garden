= Design principle

Design principles are foundational guidelines that inform decision-making in
xref:software-design.adoc[*software design*] and xref:software-architecture.adoc[*architecture*]. They codify hard-won
lessons about what tends to work and what tends to fail, offering heuristics rather than rigid rules.

A design principle expresses a preference – eg. for xref:cohesion.adoc[*cohesion*] over xref:coupling.adoc[*coupling*] –
and helps teams reason about trade-offs when competing concerns pull in different directions.

Unlike xref:design-pattern.adoc[*design patterns*], which describe reusable solutions to specific recurring problems,
design principles operate at a higher level of abstraction. They don't prescribe a particular structure or mechanism.
Instead, they shape the values and priorities that guide the selection and application of patterns. For example, the
xref:dry.adoc[*DRY*] principle argues that duplication is a source of errors and maintenance burden, but it doesn't
specify how to factor out the duplication. The xref:kiss-principle.adoc[*KISS principle*] is more direct still. It names
simplicity itself as a design goal and pushes back against the impulse to over-engineer. The xref:yagni.adoc[*YAGNI*]
principle makes the same point for features specifically — do not build for a need that has not yet arrived.

Many design principles are interrelated and mutually reinforcing. xref:cohesion.adoc[*Cohesion*] and
xref:coupling.adoc[*coupling*] are two sides of the same coin. Modules that group strongly related responsibilities tend
to depend less on other modules. xref:separation-of-concerns.adoc[*Separation of concerns*] and
xref:modular-design.adoc[*modular design*] both push toward decomposing a system into parts that can be reasoned about
independently. xref:isolation.adoc[*Isolation*] and xref:redundancy.adoc[*redundancy*] are complementary approaches to
xref:fault-tolerance.adoc[*fault tolerance*] – one limits the blast radius of failures, the other ensures that a failed
component has a backup.

Some principles are associated with particular domains or methodologies. The
xref:unix-philosophy.adoc[*Unix philosophy*] is a set of design principles for building composable command-line tools.
xref:domain-driven-design.adoc[*Domain-driven design*] advocates principles such as aligning software structure with
xref:bounded-context.adoc[*bounded contexts*]. The xref:solid.adoc[*SOLID*] principles, of which the
xref:single-responsibility-principle.adoc[*single responsibility principle*] and
xref:dependency-inversion.adoc[*dependency inversion*] are two, guide object-oriented design at the class and module
level.

Principles are most useful when they are understood in context. Applied dogmatically, any principle can lead to
*over-engineering* or unnecessary complexity. The xref:pareto-principle.adoc[*Pareto principle*] is itself a useful
reminder: a principle need not be followed perfectly to deliver most of its value.

Design principles are distinct from the broader folklore, such as xref:conways-law.adoc[*Conway's law*] and
xref:parkinsons-law.adoc[*Parkinson's law*], which tend to describe forces acting on teams and projects rather than the
shape of code itself.
