= Wirth's law

:link-wirth-paper: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/2.348001

Wirth's law is an adage on computer performance that states that software is getting slower more rapidly than hardware
is becoming faster.

It was formulated by Niklaus Wirth, the Swiss computer scientist best known for designing Pascal, in a 1995 article
titled {link-wirth-paper}[_A Plea for Lean Software_].

The law is a counterpoint to xref:moores-law.adoc[*Moore's law*]. While transistor counts double roughly every two
years, software tends to expand to consume those gains through bloat, abstraction layers, and the prioritization of
*developer experience* over *runtime efficiency*.

Wirth's law is closely related to xref:zawinskis-law.adoc[*Zawinski's law*], which observes that programs expand until
they can read email, and which has been interpreted as a comment on the phenomenon of
xref:software-bloat.adoc[*software bloat*].
