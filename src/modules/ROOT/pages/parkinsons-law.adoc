= Parkinson's law

*Parkinson's law* is the adage that "work expands so as to fill the time available for its
completion." It was coined by the British naval historian C. Northcote Parkinson in a satirical
essay published in _The Economist_ in 1955 and later expanded into his 1958 book
_Parkinson's Law: Or the Pursuit of Progress_.

The law is a wry observation about effort and deadlines. Give a task a wide window and it will
consume the whole of it; compress the window and the same task will often be finished in far less
time. The pressure of a deadline, not the inherent size of the work, sets the pace. Parkinson framed
the claim as satire, but it has been widely adopted in management science and time management as a
genuine tendency of human behavior.

Parkinson's original essay was chiefly concerned with a second, organizational form of the law: that
the staff of a public administration grows year on year regardless of the amount of work to be done.
He traced this to two forces. Officials prefer to multiply subordinates rather than rivals, and
officials generate work for one another through internal memos and review. The characteristic growth
rate he observed was 5–7% per year. This bureaucratic form of the law is the same dynamic that
xref:mythical-man-month.adoc[*Brooks's Law*] identifies from the other side: adding people to an
organization increases the communication overhead between them, and that overhead itself becomes
work.

The book also introduced the law of triviality, the observation that a committee spends its time in
inverse proportion to the importance of the item under discussion. That idea now has its own entry
as xref:bike-shedding.adoc[*bike shedding*].

Parkinson's law has acquired a family of facetious corollaries. The best known is the Stock-Sanford
corollary: "If you wait until the last minute, it only takes a minute to do." A software-flavored
variant is the observation that data expands to fill the space available for storage, which tracks
the same dynamic that drives xref:software-bloat.adoc[*software bloat*] and
xref:scope-creep.adoc[*scope creep*] in a different medium.

The practical response to Parkinson's law is to set shorter, firmer deadlines — to timebox work
rather than let it find its own level. The timeboxed iterations of methods such as
xref:scrum.adoc[*Scrum*] are partly a defense of this kind. The risk on the other side is artificial
urgency: deadlines set without regard to the real size of the work produce the
xref:ninety-ninety-rule.adoc[*ninety-ninety rule*] rather than faster delivery.
