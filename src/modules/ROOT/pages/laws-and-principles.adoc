= Laws and principles

Software development has accumulated a folklore of laws, rules, principles, and philosophies. Some are informal
observations, some are backed by data, and some are no more than adages, but they all compress hard-won lessons into a
memorable abstraction.

Some laws and principles describe forces that act on software regardless of process or technology.
xref:conways-law.adoc[*Conway's*] and xref:metcalfes-law.adoc[*Metcalfe's*] laws are examples. Others are heuristics for
individual or team behavior, such as the xref:boy-scout-rule.adoc[*boy scout rule*] and
xref:bike-shedding.adoc[*bike shedding*]. A handful, like xref:goodharts-law.adoc[*Goodhart's law*], are cautionary
tales about what happens when a measure is mistaken for the thing it measures.

The following laws and principles are distinct from xref:design-principle.adoc[*design principles*], which guide the
shape of a computer program itself. The best known example is probably the five xref:solid.adoc[*SOLID*] design
principles. The laws and principles listed below tend to operate at the scale of teams, projects, and organizations,
rather than statements, classes, and modules. But a few, such as the xref:law-of-demeter.adoc[*Law of Demeter*], sit
close to that boundary.
