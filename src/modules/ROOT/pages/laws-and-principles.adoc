= Laws and principles

Software development has accumulated a folklore of laws, rules, principles, and philosophies. Some are informal observations, some are backed by data, and some are no more than adages, but they all compress hard-won lessons into a memorable abstraction.

They vary widely in kind and in scale. Some describe forces that act on software regardless of process or technology, such as xref:conways-law.adoc[*Conway's law*] and xref:moores-law.adoc[*Moore's law*]. Others are theorems or empirical findings about systems, such as the xref:cap-theorem.adoc[*CAP theorem*] and xref:littles-law.adoc[*Little's law*]. Many are maxims for writing code and building systems, like xref:kiss-principle.adoc[*KISS*], xref:yagni.adoc[*YAGNI*], and the xref:robustness-principle.adoc[*robustness principle*], or for securing them, like the xref:principle-of-least-privilege.adoc[*principle of least privilege*].

Others are heuristics for planning and teamwork, such as the xref:boy-scout-rule.adoc[*boy scout rule*] and xref:hofstadters-law.adoc[*Hofstadter's law*]. A handful, like xref:goodharts-law.adoc[*Goodhart's law*] and xref:cargo-culting.adoc[*cargo-culting*], are cautionary tales about common ways that work goes wrong.

The following laws and principles are distinct from xref:design-principle.adoc[*design principles*], which prescribe how to structure a computer program. The best known examples are probably the five xref:solid.adoc[*SOLID*] principles. A law or principle listed below is more often a named, quotable observation or rule of thumb, which may inform design choices but also applies to process, teams, and organizations. But a few, such as xref:dry.adoc[*DRY*] and the xref:law-of-demeter.adoc[*Law of Demeter*], sit close to the boundary with design principles, because they bear directly on how code is shaped.
