= Laws and principles

Software development has accumulated a folklore of named laws, rules, and principles. Some are
informal observations, some are backed by data, and some are no more than adages, but they all
compress hard-won lessons into a memorable abstraction.

Some of the laws and principles describe forces that act on software regardless of process or
technology. xref:conways-law.adoc[*Conway*] and xref:metcalfes-law.adoc[*Metcalfe's*] laws are
examples. Others are heuristics for individual or team behavior, such as the
xref:boy-scout-rule.adoc[*boy scout rule*] and xref:bike-shedding.adoc[*bike shedding*]. A handful,
like xref:goodharts-law.adoc[*Goodhart's law*], are cautionary tales about what happens when a
measure is mistaken for the thing it measures.

These laws are distinct from xref:design-principles.adoc[*design principles*], which guide the shape
of program source code itself. The best known example is probably the xref:solid.adoc[*SOLID*]
principles. The laws and principles listed below tend to operate at the scale of teams, projects,
and organizations, rather than statements, classes, and modules. But a few, such as the
xref:law-of-demeter.adoc[*Law of Demeter*], sit close to that boundary.
