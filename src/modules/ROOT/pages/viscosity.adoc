= Viscosity

*Viscosity* is a measure of how much resistance a codebase, system, or team meets when someone tries to make a change.
It's a metaphor borrowed from physics. A low-viscosity fluid such as water flows easily, while a high-viscosity fluid
such as honey resists motion. In a high-viscosity codebase, even a small, well-understood change is slow and painful
to ship, because the design itself gets in the way.

Several things compound into that drag.

* Excess xref:coupling.adoc[*coupling*] and outdated patterns that make a small change ripple further than it should.

* Process friction, such as slow reviews, manual release steps, or unclear ownership.

* Accumulated xref:technical-debt.adoc[*technical debt*], taken on to ship quickly, which makes the next change slower still.

* Poor xref:developer-experience.adoc[*developer experience*] – weak tooling, missing documentation, inconsistent
  environments – that adds drag before a line of code is even written.

Robert C Martin named viscosity as one of the symptoms of xref:software-rot.adoc[*software rot*], alongside
xref:rigidity.adoc[*rigidity*] (where a change forces many others), xref:fragility.adoc[*fragility*] (where a change
breaks unrelated parts), and xref:immobility.adoc[*immobility*] (where a module can't be reused because it drags in
too many dependencies). Where those three describe specific failure modes, viscosity describes the felt experience of
the design as a whole. It becomes easier to hack around it than to follow the prevailing design.
