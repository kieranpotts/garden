= Design pattern

A design pattern is a general, reusable solution to a common problems in *software design*

Design patterns are not finished designs that can be translated directly into code. Rather they are templates for how to
solve a generalized problem in a way that can be adapted to many different situations.

Naming a design pattern gives a team a shared vocabulary. Saying "use an adapter here" conveys a whole design decision
in a few words.

The idea of design patterns was popularized by _Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software_ (1994),
by Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides – the "Gang of Four". Their book catalogued 23
object-oriented patterns and sorted them into three categories by intent.

* *Creational patterns* abstract the process of object construction.
* *Structural patterns* compose classes or objects into larger structures.
* *Behavioral patterns* assign responsibility and shape how objects communicate.

Creational patterns include:

* xref:factory.adoc[*Factory*]
* xref:singleton.adoc[*Singleton*]

Structural patterns include:

* xref:adapter-pattern.adoc[*Adapter pattern*]
* xref:inversion-of-control.adoc[*Inversion of control*]

Behavioral patterns include:

* xref:observer-pattern.adoc[*Observer pattern*]
* xref:publish-subscribe-pattern.adoc[*Publish-subscribe (pubsub) pattern*]
* xref:strategy-pattern.adoc[*Strategy pattern*]

The Gang of Four categories cover object-oriented design at the class and object level. Other families of design pattern
address different scales and domains. xref:architectural-pattern.adoc[*Architectural patterns*] operate on whole
systems, xref:communication-pattern.adoc[*communication patterns*] describe how components exchange data, and Martin
Fowler's *enterprise application patterns* address the structure of business applications.

xref:grasp.adoc[*GRASP*] is a smaller family focused on responsibility assignment in object-oriented designs. The
entries below collect the patterns this garden covers so far.
