= Software engineering

*Software engineering* is the discipline of building and maintaining software by systematic, disciplined, and measurable methods, in the way that established engineering fields build bridges or machines. The IEEE's standard definition calls it the application of a systematic, disciplined, quantifiable approach to the development, operation, and maintenance of software, and the study of such approaches.

The term was in circulation by the mid-1960s. Margaret Hamilton has said she began using it at MIT during the Apollo space program, to give programming the same standing as the wok on the hardware. The term entered mainstream use after the 1968 xref:nato-software-engineering-conferences.adoc[*NATO Software Engineering Conferences*], which adopted it as a deliberately provocative title. The organizers' point was that software production ought to rest on the theoretical foundations and practical disciplines found in traditional engineering. This was their proposed answer to the xref:software-crisis.adoc[*software crisis*].

Beyond writing code, the discipline spans the whole life of a system: eliciting and managing xref:requirements-engineering.adoc[*requirements*], xref:software-design.adoc[*design*], construction, xref:testing.adoc[*testing*], deployment, operation, and maintenance. It also covers the supporting practices that make that work repeatable, such as xref:configuration-management.adoc[*configuration management*], xref:software-quality-engineering.adoc[*quality engineering*], and project management. xref:swebok.adoc[*SWEBOK*] is the most complete attempt to catalogue this body of knowledge.

Whether software engineering is truly engineering is a perennial debate. Skeptics point to the lack of a stable body of physical law, and the weight of human and organizational factors. Others argue that software has its own engineering character, driven by xref:complexity.adoc[*complexity*] rather than by materials. Rival framings such as xref:software-craftsmanship.adoc[*software craftsmanship*] emphasize skill and judgment over formal methods.
