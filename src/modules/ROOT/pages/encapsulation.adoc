= Encapsulation

Encapsulation is a xref:design-patterns.adoc[*design pattern*] that is supported
by xref:object-oriented-programming.adoc[*object-oriented programming*]
languages. Encapsulation bundles data and methods that act on that data into a
single unit of code – called a class.

Encapsulation is also known as *information hiding*, a term coined by David Parnas
for the broader principle of shielding a module's internal decisions from its
callers, and is often described as *hiding implementation details*. The three
names point at the same idea: a module exposes an xref:interfaces.adoc[*interface*]
and conceals everything behind it, so that callers depend only on what the
interface promises
and not on how it is built. When that boundary fails, the result is a
xref:leaky-abstractions.adoc[*leaky abstraction*].

The purpose of encapsulation is to protect data from outside interference and
misuse, and to free the implementation to change without forcing every caller
to change with it — the discipline that
xref:encapsulate-what-changes.adoc[*encapsulating what changes*] turns into a
design rule. The xref:uniform-access-principle.adoc[*uniform access principle*]
applies the same idea to one specific decision: whether a value is stored as an
attribute or computed by a method. The xref:law-of-demeter.adoc[*Law of Demeter*]
is a complementary
discipline: it stops a caller from reaching through an object to the internals
of that object's collaborators, which would bypass the very boundary
encapsulation draws. It is the foundation that makes
xref:coupling.adoc[*loose coupling*] and xref:composition.adoc[*composition*]
practical, since both rely on callers depending on xref:abstraction.adoc[*abstractions*]
rather than on internals.

By hiding the meaning of values behind a named interface, encapsulation also
weakens xref:connascence.adoc[*connascence*], specifically connascence of
meaning, where callers would otherwise have to agree on the interpretation of a
raw value.
