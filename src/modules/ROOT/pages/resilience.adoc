= Resilience

Resilience is the ability of a system to continue functioning, in a degraded or limited capacity, in
the presence of failure – rather than failing completely. It is closely related to, but distinct
from, xref:fault-tolerance.adoc[*fault tolerance*]: fault tolerance is about preventing a failure
from causing harm at all, while resilience accepts that failures will happen and focuses on the
system's ability to absorb them and keep operating, or to recover quickly once they occur.

Resilience is a particularly important xref:quality-attributes.adoc[*quality attribute*] of
xref:distributed-system.adoc[*distributed software*], where the number of independent components –
and therefore the number of independent ways things can fail – is much higher than in a single-node
system. The xref:reactive-systems.adoc[*reactive systems*] architectural style treats resilience as
one of its four defining traits. Several design choices contribute to resilience:

* xref:isolation.adoc[*Isolation*] between components, so that a failure in one does not cascade
  into others.
* xref:circuit-breaker.adoc[*Circuit breakers*] and xref:bulkhead.adoc[*bulkheads*], which contain
  the blast radius of a failing dependency.
* xref:retry.adoc[*Retries*] with backoff, for transient failures that are likely to resolve on
  their own.
* xref:redundancy.adoc[*Redundancy*], so that a failed component can be replaced or routed around
  without the system as a whole going down.
* xref:graceful-degradation.adoc[*Graceful degradation*], so that a system whose components fail
  still delivers reduced, useful behavior rather than failing completely.
* xref:disaster-recovery.adoc[*Disaster recovery*] planning, for the failure modes that redundancy
  alone cannot absorb.

Architectures with a high degree of decoupling between components – such as
xref:peer-to-peer-architecture.adoc[*peer-to-peer networks*], which have no single point of failure,
and the xref:saga.adoc[*saga pattern*], which avoids the blocking failure modes of two-phase commit
– tend to exhibit greater resilience than tightly-coupled alternatives, at the cost of additional
design and operational complexity.

These design choices remain assumptions until they are exercised under realistic failure conditions.
xref:chaos-engineering.adoc[*Chaos engineering*] is the practice of deliberately injecting such
failures to test whether a system's resilience holds up in practice, not just on paper.
