= Recoverability

*Recoverability* is the degree to which, in the event of an interruption or a failure, a system or product can recover
the data directly affected and re-establish its desired state. ISO/IEC 25010 classes it as a sub-characteristic of
xref:reliability.adoc[*reliability*], alongside maturity, availability, and fault tolerance.

A recoverable system detects that it has failed, or that data has been lost or corrupted, and restores itself — or is
restored — to a known-good state within an acceptable time, whether through a replay of transactions, a restore from
backup, or a resynchronization from a redundant copy. This is distinct from xref:fault-tolerance.adoc[*fault tolerance*],
which is about continuing to operate despite a fault. Recoverability is about what happens once a fault has already
caused a failure.

Recoverability is usually verified through xref:recovery-testing.adoc[*recovery testing*], and is closely related to,
though narrower than, xref:disaster-recovery.adoc[*disaster recovery*], which covers the policies and procedures for
restoring an entire system's operations after a major disruptive event.
