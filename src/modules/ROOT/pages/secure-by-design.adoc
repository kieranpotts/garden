= Secure-by-design

:link-cisa:       https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/secure-by-design
:link-owasp:      https://www.owasp.org/index.php/Security_by_Design_Principles

*Secure-by-design (SbD)* is a xref:security.adoc[*security*] and systems engineering philosophy that mandates security be incorporated into systems from the outset, rather than retrofitted through patching or external controls after deployment.

The core premise is that, rather than reacting to vulnerabilities as they are discovered, secure-by-design treats security as a first-class design constraint, considered from the earliest stages of architecture and implementation alongside performance, cost, and usability.

Secure-by-design gained prominence in the early twenty-first century as high-profile incidents, such as supply chain breaches and ransomware campaigns, exposed the shortcomings of a purely reactive approach to security. Regulatory bodies and standards organizations in the US, UK, and EU now require SbD practices across domains ranging from consumer IoT to defense systems.

Secure-by-design has trade-offs. It raises up-front costs for greenfield projects, and it is hard to retrofit into legacy systems with older architectures. *Supply chain risk* is another limit. Third-party components, such as open-source libraries, may not follow the same principles, and any vulnerabilities they carry are inherited by the systems that depend on them. SbD must also be balanced against human factors. Security controls that are burdensome or poorly designed are more likely to be bypassed, which undermines their effectiveness.

== Core principles

Secure-by-design is grounded in the following principles.

* *Security as a design constraint.* Security requirements must be incorporated into the conceptual design process and upheld at every stage of subsequent design and development.

* *Assume a hostile environment.* Systems are designed on the assumption that they will be attacked. The architecture is built to make compromise difficult, contained, and recoverable.

* xref:defense-in-depth.adoc[*Defense-in-depth.*] Multiple, layered security controls are applied so that no single point of failure leads to total compromise. If one control fails, others remain.

* xref:principle-of-least-privilege.adoc[*Principle of least privilege.*] Users, processes, and services are granted only the minimum permissions necessary to perform their function. The purpose is to limit the blast radius of any compromise. xref:role-based-access-control.adoc[*Role-based access control*] is the most common design pattern in business software.

* *Minimize the attack surface.* Only necessary features, interfaces, APIs, and services are exposed. Everything else is disabled or hidden.

* Do not use xref:security-through-obscurity.adoc[*security-through-obscurity*] as a primary layer of defense, though it may be used as a supplementary one. Systems should be designed to be secure even when their internal workings are known to an adversary. See also xref:kerckhoffs-principle.adoc[*Kerckhoffs's principle*].

* *Continuous assurance.* Security measures must be continually tested, monitored, and improved, not validated once and forgotten.

== Relationship to related paradigms

Secure-by-design overlaps with and complements several related concepts.

* *Privacy-by-design.* Extends the same "build it in from the start" philosophy to the protection of personal data and user privacy. See xref:privacy.adoc[*privacy*].

* *Zero-trust architecture.* A security model that treats every request as potentially hostile, requiring continuous verification rather than relying on network perimeter controls. Zero trust is an expression of the "assume a hostile environment" principle applied at the architectural level.

* xref:shift-left.adoc[*Shift left.*] The broader software development practice of moving quality and security concerns earlier in the development lifecycle.

== Methodologies and frameworks

Secure-by-design is a set of principles, not a particular method or tool. Frameworks that operationalize SbD principles include the following.

* *Microsoft Security Development Lifecycle (SDL).* Integrates security activities such as threat modeling, code review, and xref:penetration-testing.adoc[*penetration testing*] into every phase of product development.

* *NIST SP 800-160.* A systems security engineering framework from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology, oriented toward building intrinsically secure and resilient systems.

* *Threat modeling.* A structured approach to identifying threats, attack vectors, and mitigations during the design phase, before code is written.

* xref:owasp.adoc[*OWASP Secure Design Principles*] offer practical, code-level guidance.

* *SEI Secure Design Patterns* (Carnegie Mellon, 2009). Reusable strategies for solving common security design problems.
