= Association

An *association* is a relationship between two software components in which one component uses, depends on, or otherwise 
has knowledge of the other. It is the most general of the relationships between classes in
xref:object-oriented-programming.adoc[*object-oriented programming*], and the one from which the more specific
xref:composition.adoc[*composition*] and *aggregation* relationships are derived.

An association is typically realized as one object holding a reference to another, either to a concrete class or through
an xref:interface.adoc[*interface*]. The holder can invoke the collaborator's operations, and the collaborator may in
turn hold a reference back, forming a bidirectional association. 

Directionality and multiplicity — one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many — are what distinguishes one type of association 
from another.

The xref:uml.adoc[*Unified Modeling Language*] notation represents association as a solid line drawn between two classes 
in a class diagram. The line may carry multiplicities at each end (eg. `1`, `0..1`, `*`), role names describing each 
end's purpose, and an open arrowhead indicating navigability. A line with no arrowheads denotes a bidirectional 
association.

In UML, there are two refined forms of association.

* *Aggregation.* A "has a" relationship in which the part can exist independently of the whole. It is the strongest
  form of association. It is drawn with a  hollow diamond at the whole end.

* *Composition.* A stronger "owns a" relationship in which the part's lifetime is bound to the whole. When the whole is
  destroyed, so are its parts. It is drawn with a filled diamond at the whole end.

Association is distinct from inheritance. xref:inheritance.adoc[*Inheritance*] expresses an "is a" relationship between 
classes. Association expresses a "uses a" or "has a" relationship. The two are often contrasted. Inheritance ties a 
subclass to its parent's implementation at compile time, while an association lets a class reach a collaborator through 
a reference that can be swapped at runtime (when the reference is held behind an interface). 

Composition is recommended over inheritance for code reuse, because it keeps xref:coupling.adoc[*coupling*] lower and 
behaviors replaceable.
