= Access patterns

A key consideration in *xref:system-design.adoc[system design]* is the access
patterns of the data.

Access patterns describe how data is read and written, and how it is queried.
They capture the ratio of reads to writes, the shapes of the queries, the
frequency with which particular records are touched, and the locality of those
accesses over time. Systems may be read-heavy or write-heavy, or have a
balanced mix of reads and writes. The access patterns of a system are
influenced by its *xref:domain.adoc[domain]* and *xref:requirements.adoc[requirements]* such as
*xref:scalability.adoc[scalability]*, *xref:availability.adoc[availability]*,
and *xref:consistency.adoc[consistency]*.

Several dimensions characterize a system's access patterns.

* *Read/write ratio.* Whether the workload is dominated by reads, writes, or a
  balance of both.
* *Query shape.* Point lookups by key, range scans over ordered data,
  aggregations, or graph traversals.
* *Access locality.* Whether requests concentrate on a small working set of
  hot records or spread evenly across the data.
* *Temporal patterns.* Bursts of traffic, diurnal cycles, or event-driven
  spikes.

These dimensions drive many downstream design decisions. A read-heavy workload
favors *xref:database-indexes.adoc[indexes]*, *xref:caching.adoc[caching]*, and
*xref:materialized-view.adoc[materialized views]* that accelerate reads at the
cost of slower or more complex writes. A write-heavy workload favors
append-only storage and *xref:sharding.adoc[sharding]* to distribute write
load. Where read and write patterns diverge sharply,
*xref:cqrs.adoc[command query responsibility segregation (CQRS)]* separates the
read and write models so each can be optimized independently. The choice of
*xref:data-modeling.adoc[data model]* and storage technology follows the same
logic. *xref:relational-databases.adoc[Relational databases]* suit ad-hoc
queries and complex joins, while *xref:nosql-databases.adoc[NoSQL databases]*
are often selected for their fit to a specific, well-understood access pattern
such as key-value lookup or wide-column reads.

Access patterns can have a significant influence on the
*xref:architecture-and-design.adoc[design]* of a system, but the patterns may
not be fully understood until the system is in production. Real workloads often
reveal *hot spots*, uneven concentrations of traffic, that were not visible in
design-time assumptions. Therefore, optimizing the system design for its access
patterns is a challenging constraint and may require iterative design
improvements. *xref:load-testing.adoc[Load testing]* and production
*xref:observability.adoc[observability]* help surface the true patterns so the
design can be refined.
