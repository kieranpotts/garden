= Zawinski's law

Zawinski's law holds that *every program attempts to expand until it can read mail; those programs which cannot so expand are replaced by ones which can*.
It is attributed to Jamie Zawinski, an early Netscape and Mozilla developer,
who made the remark in a 1990s Usenet discussion.

The law is a satirical exaggeration of a real tendency: successful software
tends to accumulate features well beyond its original scope, as each new
capability seems individually reasonable to the team maintaining it, and as
users push for the program to absorb functionality that would otherwise
require switching to a different tool. Email reading was, at the time
Zawinski coined the remark, an emblematic example of scope creep, a feature
that seemed to turn up eventually in text editors, IDEs, and other tools that
had no obvious business handling mail.

The law is closely related to xref:wirths-law.adoc[*Wirth's law*] and is
frequently cited as a comment on xref:software-bloat.adoc[*software bloat*]:
the tendency of long-lived software to grow heavier and more complex over
successive releases as features accumulate, even when no single addition
looks unreasonable on its own.
