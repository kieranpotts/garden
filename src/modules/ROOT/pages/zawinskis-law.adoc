= Zawinski's law

Zawinski's law holds that every computer program attempts to expand in capability until it can read
email, and those programs that cannot so expand are eventually replaced by ones that can.

The law is attributed to Jamie Zawinski, an early Netscape and Mozilla developer, who made the
remark in a 1990s Usenet discussion.

The law is a satirical exaggeration of a real tendency for successful software to accumulate
features well beyond its original scope. Users push for new functionality that would otherwise
require them to switch to an alternative program, and each feature request seems individually
reasonable to the team maintaining the program. Email reading was, at the time, an emblematic
example of such xref:scope-creep.adoc[*scope creep*], turning up in all sorts of tools that had
no obvious business handling mail.

The law is a comment on the phenomenon of xref:software-bloat.adoc[*software bloat*] and is closely
related to xref:wirths-law.adoc[*Wirth's law*].
