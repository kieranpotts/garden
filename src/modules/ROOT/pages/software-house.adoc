= Software house

A *software house* is a service-based company that builds custom software for other organizations. The client commissions an outcome, such as a web platform or a mobile app, and the software house takes ownership of delivering it, typically covering discovery, design, development, testing, project management, and post-launch support under one contract. The terms *software development agency* and *tech consultancy* are used interchangeably with it.

A software house is the opposite of a product company. It sells its team's time and expertise to produce bespoke systems, whereas a product company builds software once and sells it to many customers, as with xref:shrinkwrap-software.adoc[*shrinkwrap software*] or xref:software-as-a-service.adoc[*software-as-a-service*].

Commissioning a software house is one of several ways to get software built. Compared with an in-house team, it avoids the cost and lead time of hiring, but gives the client less control and less accumulated knowledge of the system. Compared with freelancers, it brings a ready-made xref:cross-functional-team.adoc[*cross-functional team*] and a single accountable party. It also differs from an *IT staffing agency*, which supplies individual developers who work under the client's direction, often augmenting their own delivery teams, so that the client buys capacity rather than delivery.

Work is usually priced as either *fixed-price* or *time-and-materials*. Fixed-price contracts give the client a predictable budget but handle changing scope badly, and suit a plan-driven approach such as the xref:waterfall-model.adoc[*waterfall model*]. Time-and-materials contracts absorb change more easily, and fit iterative, xref:agile.adoc[*agile*] delivery. (The tension between fixed scope, cost, and schedule is the subject of the xref:project-paradox.adoc[*project paradox*].)

Software houses are often described by where they sit relative to the client: _onshore_ in the same country, _nearshore_ in a neighboring region with overlapping working hours, or _offshore_ across a large time-zone gap. Lower rates offshore trade off against the cost of communication.
