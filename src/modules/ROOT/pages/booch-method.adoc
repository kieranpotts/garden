= Booch method

The Booch method is an object-oriented analysis and design (OOAD) method
developed by Grady Booch in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and set out in his
book _Object-Oriented Design with Applications_ (1991, with a substantially
revised second edition in 1994). It was one of the most influential of the
first generation of xref:object-oriented-programming.adoc[*object-oriented programming*] design methods, alongside James Rumbaugh's Object Modeling
Technique (OMT) and Ivar Jacobson's Object-Oriented Software Engineering
(OOSE).

The method couples a graphical notation for representing
xref:modeling.adoc[*models*] of a system with a process for producing them. Its
notation covers both the *micro* level – classes and objects, their
relationships, and state transition diagrams – and the *macro* level of
modules and subsystems that group those classes. Class boxes in Booch's
original notation were drawn as distinctive "cloud" shapes, a signature that
now survives mainly in historical references since the notation itself has
been superseded.

== Process

Booch described his process as iterative and incremental rather than as a
single waterfall pass. He distinguished between a *macro process* – the
long-arc evolution of a system's architecture across releases – and a *micro process* of day-to-day class discovery and refinement. The macro process
identifies candidate classes and the
xref:software-architecture.adoc[*software architecture*] early, then revisits
analysis and design in successive cycles.

This places the method somewhere alongside
xref:big-design-up-front.adoc[*big design up-front*] on the spectrum of
up-front design, although Booch himself argued against treating the
architecture as a one-off artifact to be settled and set aside. He was a
vocal advocate for keeping architecture central throughout development.

== Legacy

In 1994 Booch joined James Rumbaugh and Ivar Jacobson at Rational Software,
where the three – known as the "three amigos" – unified their competing
notations into the xref:uml.adoc[*Unified Modeling Language*]. UML adopted
much of the Booch notation, recasting its cloud class boxes as rectangles.
Booch's process thinking, combined with that of his colleagues, subsequently
shaped the xref:unified-process.adoc[*Unified Process*] and its commercial
realization, the
xref:rational-unified-process.adoc[*Rational Unified Process*].

Rational's later modeling tool, Rational Software Modeler, carried this
lineage forward after IBM acquired Rational in 2003. It was eventually
superseded by IBM Rational Software Architect and other products in IBM's
engineering tooling suite.
