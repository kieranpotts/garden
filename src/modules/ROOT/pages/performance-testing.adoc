= Performance testing

Performance testing is a category of software testing concerned with evaluating how well a system behaves under various
conditions, verifying that it meets the xref:performance.adoc[*performance*] criteria specified in the user's
requirements.

Rather than checking functional correctness, performance testing focuses on qualities such as responsiveness,
xref:throughput.adoc[*throughput*], stability, and resource utilization. These are among the system's
xref:non-functional-requirements.adoc[*non-functional requirements*]. Performance testing may be used to ensure that an
application performs not just under ideal conditions, but across a range of realistic and demanding scenarios.

Two of the most important techniques within performance testing are xref:load-testing.adoc[*load testing*] and
xref:stress-testing.adoc[*stress testing*], each probing the system's limits in a distinct way. Load testing examines
system behavior when subjected to higher-than-anticipated volumes of traffic or transactions, pushing the system beyond
its expected operational limits to observe how performance degrades and at what point failures occur. Stress testing
takes a similarly aggressive approach, subjecting the system to conditions beyond its normal operational capacity –
whether in terms of concurrent users, data volume, or processing demand – in order to identify breaking points and
assess how gracefully the system fails and recovers.

Performance testing techniques give development and operations teams a clearer picture of where bottlenecks lie, what
headroom exists before degradation sets in, and whether the system can meet its performance obligations under the
pressures of real-world use.

Once the system is in production, these same qualities are tracked under real traffic by
xref:application-performance-management.adoc[*application performance management*], the production-side counterpart to
performance testing.
