= Kerckhoffs's principle

Kerckhoffs's principle states that a cryptographic system should be secure even if everything about the system is public knowledge. The principle was formulated by the Dutch cryptographer and linguist Auguste Kerckhoffs in his 1883 essay _La Cryptographie Militaire_ as one of six design requirements for military cryptosystems.

The central observation is that a cipher whose strength depends on its algorithm remaining secret fails to be a xxxxxx as soon as that algorithm leaks or is reverse-engineered.

Modern cryptography treats the algorithm as public. Modern cryptographic standards, such as AES and the algorithms behind TLS, are published in the open and subjected to public scrutiny by cryptanalysts.
