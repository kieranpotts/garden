= NATO Software Engineering Conferences

The *NATO Software Engineering Conferences* were two meetings, in 1968 and 1969, sponsored by the NATO Science Committee, that brought together academics and industry practitioners to confront the xref:software-crisis.adoc[*software crisis*]. They are widely regarded as the founding events of xref:software-engineering.adoc[*software engineering*] as a recognized discipline. That phrase was chosen for the first conference's title as a deliberate provocation, to suggest that software production needed the same theoretical foundations and practical discipline of established engineering fields.

== Garmisch, 1968

The first conference was held in Garmisch, Germany, from 7 to 11 October 1968, with Friedrich L. Bauer as chair. Dozens of participants from across Europe and North America attended, and they spoke frankly about failed projects, schedule and cost overruns, and the inability of existing methods to scale.

Doug McIlroy's proposal for mass-produced software components, an early statement of the case for xref:code-reuse.adoc[*reuse*], was one of the notable contributions.

== Rome, 1969

The second conference followed in Rome from 27 to 31 October 1969, with the final report published in 1970. Where Garmisch had diagnosed the problem, Rome was meant to examine the techniques that might solve it.

The mood at this conference was more divided, with participants differing over whether progress was being made, and that tension was visible in the report.

The series ended there, and no further conferences were held.
