= PACELC theorem

:link-abadi-paper: https://www.cs.umd.edu/~abadi/papers/abadi-pacelc.pdf

The PACELC theorem extends the xref:cap-theorem.adoc[*CAP theorem*] to describe the trade-offs faced by
xref:distributed-system.adoc[*distributed software*] that replicates data. It was formulated by Daniel Abadi in 2010, in
response to a weakness in the CAP model.

The CAP theorem only addresses what happens when a network xref:partition-tolerance.adoc[*partition*] occurs: the system
must then choose between xref:consistency.adoc[*consistency*] and xref:availability.adoc[*availability*]. But partitions
are comparatively rare. For the rest of the time, while the system runs normally, CAP has nothing to say. Yet a
replicated store still has to make a trade-off — between the xref:latency.adoc[*latency*] of its requests and the
consistency of its replicas — because keeping replicas in lockstep requires coordination that adds latency to every
write and often to every read.

PACELC names both cases. If a partition occurs (P), the system chooses availability (A) or consistency (C). Else, when
there is no partition (E), it chooses latency (L) or consistency (C). The two choices are independent, so a system is
described by a pair, eg. PA/EL or PC/EC.

* *PA/EL.* Under a partition the system favors availability, and in normal operation it favors low latency. This is the
  most common choice among xref:nosql-database.adoc[*NoSQL databases*] descended from Amazon Dynamo, such as Cassandra and
  Voldemort. They serve requests from any replica without coordination and accept
  xref:eventual-consistency.adoc[*eventual consistency*].

* *PC/EC.* Under a partition the system favors consistency, and in normal operation it also favors consistency over
  latency. Stores built on xref:consensus-algorithm.adoc[*consensus algorithms*], such as Bigtable, HBase, and Spanner,
  sit here. They block or fail requests rather than serve stale data, and they pay higher latency even when the network is
  healthy.

* *PA/EC and PC/EL.* The crossed combinations are possible but uncommon. PNUTS, a Yahoo research store, chose availability
  under partitions but consistency in normal operation (PA/EC). PC/EL — consistent under partitions, latency first
  otherwise — is rarer still, since the same coordination machinery that preserves consistency during a partition usually
  governs normal operation too.

PACELC's value is descriptive rather than prescriptive. It does not say which choice is correct, and it does not address
other qualities such as xref:throughput.adoc[*throughput*] or cost. Its point is that the consistency trade-off is not
an emergency measure reserved for partitions. It is a standing decision a replicated system makes every moment it is
running, and CAP hides that behind the partition case.
