= Evaluation

*Evaluation*, in software architecture, is the practice of assessing whether a system or its architecture can meet its
desired or required xref:quality-attribute.adoc[*quality attributes*]. It comes in two complementary forms.

*Qualitative evaluation* is concerned with finding risks against a system's desired qualities, rather than calculating
or measuring anything directly. It works by structured judgment, surfacing xref:risk.adoc[*risks*], trade-offs, and
xref:sensitivity-point.adoc[*sensitivity points*] in an architecture.

The xref:architecture-tradeoff-analysis-method.adoc[*Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method (ATAM)*] is the best-known
method for doing this systematically.

*Quantitative evaluation* (or quantitative analysis) measures or counts values of software artifacts, such as
xref:coupling.adoc[*coupling*], xref:cyclomatic-complexity.adoc[*cyclomatic complexity*], size, or test coverage.
xref:metrics.adoc[*Metrics*] like these help identify critical or risky parts of a system, complementing the risks a
qualitative evaluation surfaces with hard numbers.
