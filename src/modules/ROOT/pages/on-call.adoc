= On-call

On-call is a practice in which designated engineers are available outside normal working hours –
xref:away-from-keyboard.adoc[*away from keyboard*] but reachable – to respond to incidents affecting a production
system. When xref:alerting.adoc[*alerts*] fire – indicating degraded service, errors, or outages – the on-call engineer
is responsible for investigating, triaging, and either resolving the issue or escalating it. The practice is a core
component of xref:site-reliability-engineering.adoc[*site reliability engineering*] and xref:devops.adoc[*DevOps*],
ensuring that someone is always accountable for system health.

An on-call rotation distributes this responsibility across a team so that no individual is perpetually burdened.
Rotations may follow various schedules – weekly, biweekly, or split into primary and secondary tiers – and typically
distinguish between weekday and weekend coverage. The goal is to balance rapid response times against the risk of
*alert fatigue*, where excessive or noisy alerts desensitize engineers and degrade the quality of responses.

Alert thresholds are typically derived from xref:service-level-agreement.adoc[*service level agreement*] targets, so
that an on-call page signals a real risk of breaching a commitment to users.

Effective on-call depends on good xref:observability.adoc[*observability*]. Engineers need xref:logging.adoc[*logs*],
xref:tracing.adoc[*traces*], xref:metrics.adoc[*metrics*], and xref:runbook.adoc[*runbooks*] to diagnose problems
quickly under pressure. Conversely, on-call experience feeds back into the system: recurring incidents expose weak
points in the architecture, and the insights gained should drive improvements that reduce future alert volume.

On-call is one of many conventions that make up a team's xref:ways-of-working.adoc[*ways of working*]. It intersects
with xref:alerting.adoc[*alerting*] strategy, incident management processes, and post-incident review practices.
