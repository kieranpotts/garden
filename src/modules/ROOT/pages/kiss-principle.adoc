= KISS principle

*KISS – "keep it simple, stupid"* – is a xref:design-principle.adoc[*design principles*] that states that simplicity
should be a primary goal of design. It's a reminder to reach for the simplest solution that meets the requirement, and
a check against the urge to *over-engineer*.

KISS is not specific to software. It originated in aerospace engineering and has been adopted across design,
communication, and management. In software it is a counterweight to xref:complexity.adoc[*accidental complexity*],
which is the complexity that comes from the choices a system's designers made, rather than from the problem itself.
Every abstraction, indirection, and speculative feature is a form of accidental complexity.

Simplicity in this sense is not the same as being simplistic. A simple solution can still be sophisticated. The goal
is the minimal xref:abstraction.adoc[*abstraction*] that meets the need, not the shallowest one. The principle
constrains the design space, not the quality bar.

Like any principle, KISS misleads when applied dogmatically. "Simple" is a judgment relative to an audience and a context,
and what is simple to one reader is opaque to another. The principle is most useful as a question – "is there a simpler
thing that would work here?" – rather than as a license to cut corners.
