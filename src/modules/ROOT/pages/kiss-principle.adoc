= KISS principle

*KISS – "keep it simple, stupid"* – is a design principle that holds that simplicity should be a primary goal of design,
and that unnecessary complexity should be avoided. Most systems work best when they are kept simple rather than made
complicated, and the principle is a reminder to reach for the simplest solution that meets the requirement. The
principle argues against the urge to *over-engineer*.

KISS is not specific to software. It originated in aerospace engineering and has been adopted across design,
communication, and management. In software it functions as a counterweight to xref:complexity.adoc[*accidental complexity*],
which is the complexity a system exhibits as a result of the choices its designers made, rather being necessary to solve
the problem itself. Every abstraction, indirection, and speculative feature is a form of accidental complexity.

Simplicity in this sense is not the same as being simplistic. A simple solution can still be sophisticated. The goal is
the minimal xref:abstraction.adoc[*abstraction*] that meets the need, not the shallowest one. The principle constrains
the design space, not the quality bar. A solution that is simple but wrong, or simple but unreadable, is not what KISS
is asking for. Once a simple solution is proven, xref:refactoring.adoc[*refactoring*] can deepen it as understanding
grows.

KISS is one of a family of pragmatic xref:design-principle.adoc[*design principles*] that point the same way.
xref:yagni.adoc[*YAGNI*] warns against building for hypothetical needs. xref:dry.adoc[*DRY*] warns against duplicating
knowledge. The xref:do-the-simplest-thing.adoc[*do the simplest thing*] heuristic asks for the minimal solution that
could possibly work. xref:worse-is-better.adoc[*Worse is better*] makes a related but distinct claim, that simplicity of
implementation matters more than simplicity of interface, and that incomplete-but-simple systems tend to outlive
complete-but-complex ones. The xref:rule-of-least-power.adoc[*rule of least power*] extends the same instinct to the
choice of language, preferring the least expressive language that still solves the problem. The
xref:unix-philosophy.adoc[*Unix philosophy*] applies the same instinct to small composable tools, and the
xref:grug-brained-developer.adoc[*grug-brained developer*] persona is its popular caricature.

Like any principle, KISS misleads when applied dogmatically. "Simple" is a judgment about a specific audience and
context, and what is simple to one reader is opaque to another. The principle is most useful as a question – "is there a
simpler thing that would work here?" – rather than a license to cut corners.
