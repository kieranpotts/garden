= Design principles

Design principles are foundational guidelines that inform decision-making in
software design. They codify hard-won lessons about what tends to work and what
tends to fail, offering heuristics rather than rigid rules.

A design principle expresses a preference – eg. for
*xref:cohesion.adoc[cohesion]* over *xref:coupling.adoc[coupling]* – and helps
teams reason about trade-offs when competing concerns pull in different
directions.

Unlike *xref:design-patterns.adoc[design patterns]*, which describe reusable
solutions to specific recurring problems, design principles operate at a higher
level of abstraction. They don't prescribe a particular structure or mechanism.
Instead, they shape the values and priorities that guide the selection and
application of patterns. For example, the *xref:dry.adoc[DRY]* principle argues
that duplication is a source of errors and maintenance burden, but it doesn't
specify how to factor out the duplication – that is left to the designer's
judgment.

Many design principles are interrelated and mutually reinforcing.
*xref:cohesion.adoc[Cohesion]* and *xref:coupling.adoc[coupling]* are two sides
of the same coin. Modules that group strongly related responsibilities tend to
depend less on other modules.
*xref:separation-of-concerns.adoc[Separation of concerns]* and
*xref:modular-design.adoc[modular design]* both push toward decomposing a system
into parts that can be reasoned about independently.
*xref:isolation.adoc[Isolation]* and *xref:redundancy.adoc[redundancy]* are
complementary approaches to *xref:fault-tolerance.adoc[fault tolerance]* – one
limits the blast radius of failures, the other ensures that a failed component
has a backup.

Some principles are associated with particular domains or methodologies. The
*xref:unix-philosophy.adoc[Unix philosophy]* is a set of design principles for
building composable command-line tools.
*xref:domain-driven-design.adoc[Domain-driven design]* advocates principles such
as aligning software structure with
*xref:bounded-context.adoc[bounded contexts]*. The SOLID principles, of which
*xref:dependency-inversion.adoc[dependency inversion]* is one, guide
object-oriented design at the class and module level.

Principles are most useful when they are understood in context. Applied
dogmatically, any principle can lead to *over-engineering* or unnecessary
complexity. The *xref:pareto-principle.adoc[Pareto principle]* is itself a
useful reminder: a principle need not be followed perfectly to deliver most of
its value.
