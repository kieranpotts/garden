= Ricing

*Ricing* is the practice of heavily customizing the look and feel of a desktop environment, most often on xref:linux.adoc[*Linux*] and other Unix-like systems. A customized setup is called a *rice*, and the person building it is *ricing* their desktop. Ricing is a hobbyist activity, since the aim is usually a personal, visually distinctive environment rather than any measurable gain in performance or functionality.

Typical customizations include replacing the default desktop environment with a standalone *window manager* – often a tiling one such as i3, bspwm, or Sway, or a compositor such as Hyprland – and then assembling the surrounding pieces by hand: a status bar, application launcher, notification daemon, and compositor effects. On top of that sit color schemes, icon and GTK/Qt themes, fonts, wallpapers, and the styling of the xref:shell.adoc[*shell*] prompt and terminal emulator. Most of this is controlled through plain-text configuration files, so a rice is easy to version and share. Practitioners habitually publish these *dotfiles* in public Git repositories for others to borrow from.

The community hub for ricing is the `r/unixporn` subreddit, where members post screenshots of their desktops, usually with a link to the dotfiles that produced them.

The term is borrowed from car culture, where "rice" or "ricer" was a slang term for cheap cosmetic modifications to an import car – spoilers, decals, and body kits – that made it look fast without making it any faster. The car slang is widely regarded as a racial slur, derived from a stereotype about Asian people, and some users avoid the computing term for that reason.
