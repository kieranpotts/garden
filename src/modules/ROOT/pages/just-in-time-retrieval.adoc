= Just-in-time retrieval

*Just-in-time retrieval* is the practice of fetching information at the moment it is needed, rather than gathering it all in advance. The consumer holds lightweight references to the information, such as identifiers, file paths, links, or stored queries, and resolves them into full content on demand. It is the same idea as xref:lazy-loading.adoc[*lazy loading*], applied to knowledge rather than to program resources, and it echoes just-in-time manufacturing, where parts arrive as they are needed instead of being stockpiled.

Retrieving on demand avoids the cost of loading, holding, and keeping current material that may never be used, and whatever is retrieved is fresh at the point of use. The trade-off is that the consumer must know what to look for and when, increasing the volume of conditional logic required in the consumer. Each retrieval also adds latency and depends on the source being available. Preloading remains the better choice for material that is small, stable, and needed almost every time.

In systems built on large language models, just-in-time retrieval is a core technique of xref:context-engineering.adoc[*context engineering*]. The context window is finite, and filling it with marginally relevant material wastes tokens and invites xref:large-language-model.adoc[*context rot*]. So rather than preloading everything, an xref:agent-harness.adoc[*agent harness*] gives the model tools to search and read, and the model pulls in content as the task demands. Searching a codebase for the one file that matters, instead of loading the whole repository, is the typical use case. xref:agent-memory.adoc[*Progressive disclosure*] is a closely related pattern, where a short description is always loaded, and the full body is loaded only when triggered, as with xref:agent-skills.adoc[*agent skills*]. The cost is that each retrieval is an extra step in the xref:agent-loop.adoc[*agent loop*].
