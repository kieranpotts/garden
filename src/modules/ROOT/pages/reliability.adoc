= Reliability

*Reliability* is the degree to which a system, product, or component performs its specified functions under specified
conditions for a specified period of time. It is one of the xref:quality-attribute.adoc[*quality attributes*] defined
by ISO/IEC 25010, and one of the qualities most directly associated with user trust. An unreliable system may have
every feature a user needs, yet still fail to satisfy them if it can't be counted on to work reliably.

ISO/IEC 25010 composes reliability from four sub-characteristics: xref:maturity.adoc[*maturity*] (performing
reliably under normal operation), xref:availability.adoc[*availability*] (being operational and accessible when
needed), xref:fault-tolerance.adoc[*fault tolerance*] (continuing to operate despite faults), and
xref:recoverability.adoc[*recoverability*] (re-establishing the desired state after an interruption or failure).

Reliability engineering typically works across all four. Redundancy and graceful degradation raise fault tolerance and
availability, while good backup, replay, and resynchronization mechanisms raise recoverability. Because these
sub-characteristics interact, improving one in isolation — for example, adding retries without also handling the
partial failures they can mask — can leave overall reliability little improved, or even worse.
